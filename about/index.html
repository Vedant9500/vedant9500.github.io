About

Computer engineering student in India, publishing as Colossen. I build things from scratch and figure out why they don’t work.

Background

I got into programming through machine learning, then got pulled into the systems underneath it: the solvers, the benchmarks, and the tools that tell you how things actually work (or why they’re breaking).

I find something I only understand as a black box, rebuild the smallest version I can measure, then spend a while figuring out why it disagrees with what I expected. That’s usually the part I enjoy most.

What I work on

Most of it falls into four lanes.

Discovery
Glassbox: recovering equations from raw data.
Simulation
FMMS, a fast-multipole N-body solver in Rust; CelestialSim, RK4 in the browser.
Measurement
freeze-bench, an execution-graded benchmark for coding models.
Tooling
Hark, WTF, and OpenLMlib, things I wanted for my own workflow.

Stack

Rust for anything that has to stay running or stay fast: FMMS, Hark. C++ where Glassbox needs the inner loop. Python for the parts that change every day. Go for CLIs.

Off-screen

F1 and most motorsports, fighter jets, rocket engines, and CFD. In all of them the appeal is the same: something moves fast enough that you have to simulate it to see what happens. I daily-drive Arch, which is mostly an excuse to keep rebuilding my desktop.
